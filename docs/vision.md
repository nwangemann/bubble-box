# Vision

Zen simplicity and beauty. Every action is simple, organic and calm.

## The interaction

1. Touch: a bubble begins under the finger, against the glass.
2. Hold: it grows steadily, always slowing, and breathes. It follows the finger with a soft lag. Every finger grows its own.
3. Release: it leaves the glass into the box. A longer hold launches harder; a swipe steers; a larger bubble turns less readily. A curved swipe spins it, and its path keeps curving.
4. It meets a wall, squashes, sends a ripple across it and rebounds, eventually back to the viewer.
5. Meeting the glass sends a gentle refracting ripple across the screen (the Glass option).
6. Bubbles that meet throw light shaped by the collision (the Meet option).
7. A quick two-finger swipe pops what it crosses (the Pop option chooses how). A tap can also pop (the Tap option). A pop breathes on its neighbours: they drift away from it, and held ones shiver. Once, two faint fingertips act it out, for whoever has not found it.
8. A quick swipe that starts on a bubble flicks it: it is nudged that way, and spins if caught near its edge.
9. Bubbles that touch can merge (the Contact option): edges swell into a neck and the smaller sinks into the larger.
10. Bubbles can draw each other (the Pull option): paths bend, and some meet that would have missed.
11. Bubbles can leave a trail (the Trail option): it narrows and fades with age and distance, and folds where they bounce.
12. Tilting the phone lets bubbles drift gently to the low side, until the hand settles into its new hold.

## Showdown

The one part that is a show. Chosen with the mode switch in the menu, whose sides look like what they lead to, it covers the room, which waits beneath, and one dot leaves. Touch does nothing while it runs, except in the line-up, where a tap chooses a favourite to root for, marked by a soft light. Its arenas are see-through grid boxes like the room, so depth shows; scenery is only a ghost on the back wall. Themes are playful; bubbles and how they meet are realistic: films that bend light, a neck that opens and drains, a surface that rings and settles. Names, the winner's title and a quiet count of crowns and right calls are the exception to having no text; nothing counts against anyone.

## 8-ball

A trial of play rather than watching: pool in the same box, the far wall holding six pockets. The cue ball is grown and sent as a calm bubble is; the player always knows where each ball is from perspective, shadows and light on the walls. Turns, groups and the win are shown without words, by a row of balls and a line under whose half it is. Design and rules are in [pool](pool.md). Kept only if it is fun on a phone.

## Spirit

A solo, experimental project in which artistic play decides. Gestures should be intuitive and found without instructions. Ideas are explored widely, then reduced to what works. Reduce Motion and VoiceOver are supported, but the interaction is designed first for touch and sight.

## Principles

- The bubble is the interface. One dot is the only permanent control.
- No scores, text or sound that asks for attention.
- Motion eases. Nothing snaps, flashes or stops dead.
- Responsiveness first: touch response beats visual richness.
- Light on battery: an empty room draws nothing.

## Glossary

- **Box**: the room. Its near face is the screen.
- **Glass**: that near face.
- **Held**: a bubble still under the finger.
- **Charge**: 0 to 1, how far a hold has progressed.
- **Burst**: the light from a meeting or a pop. **Style**: how a bubble looks. **Environment**: how the box looks and bounces. **Feel**: a physics preset.
